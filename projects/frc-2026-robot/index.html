FRC 2026 Robot

FIRST Robotics Team 2137's robot for the 2026 season, Downpour.

Robot Overview

The 2026 FIRST Robotics Competition game challenged teams to store many 15 cm foam balls, bring them to their side of the field, and launch them into the goal as fast as possible. Team 2137’s robot, which I designed, can hold 85 balls. It is also capable of shooting while moving from anywhere on the field at a rate of about 7 balls per second. Our competition performance was excellent, being finalists at both of our district events, and placing third at the FIRST in Michigan District Championship.

Design Process

At the start of the season, we thought about and prototyped different robot designs and strategies. After evaluating each of them based on their difficulty and effectiveness, we decided to leverage our team’s software capability by creating a turreted launcher.

With such an integrated robot design, it was necessary to take time to thoroughly plan each mechanism’s design and how they integrate with each other. To do this, I created simplified models of most components to visualize the interactions between each mechanism. After creating this block CAD, I imported it into separate documents to create the final robot design. Though creating the block CAD took substantial effort, it sped up the creation of the full-detail design work significantly, saving us time in the long run.

Iteration

Creating a complex robot isn't easy. Not everything works perfectly on the first attempt.

At our first event, the bottom roller on our intake would get stuck while deploying if there were balls under it. To fix this, we changed the deployment method so that the bottom roller deployed once at the beginning of the match, then stayed down.

Our original hopper design left much to be desired. It wasn’t thought out very well, it broke frequently, and it was ugly. Additionally, the extendable portion was attached to the intake. So, whenever the intake tried to retract to agitate the balls, it had to move a ton of balls. After our first event, we entirely redesigned our hopper with thicker, darker material, stronger joints, and a roof that prevented balls from falling out. The extendable portion was no longer attached to the intake, and was permanently deployed at the start of the match.
