Manufacturing

My journey of learning manufacturing in high school classes and on the robotics team.

Manual Machines

My first experience with manufacturing was creating component drawings to communicate dimensions to the fabrication team. I wasn’t perfect at first, but after years on the team, I learned how to create drawings that clearly communicate manufacturing information. In college, I hope to learn more about manufacturing tolerances and how they affect component drawings.

Design for Manufacturing

While DFM is simple in principle, applying it can be complex. Compromises are often needed so components are both effective and machinable. Discussions with the team’s fabrication subteam taught me which features are easy, difficult, or impossible to machine. My CNC experience has also given me practice designing complex parts that need multiple setups while staying practical to manufacture.

CNC Machines

CNC Routers

Starting with little guidance, I learned to program operations in SOLIDWORKS CAM and find router settings that kept tools from breaking. I used those skills to make a significant portion of the team’s 2024 robot. After that router broke in 2025, the school got a newer machine in 2026, and I moved to Fusion 360. I built templates for our common operations, so even complex parts like this hood gear take only minutes to program.

CNC Mills

Robot parts rarely needed the school’s Tormach mill, but I wanted to learn it. After the routers, the mill was easy to pick up. Working four hours a week, I made this sprocket in a few weeks.

Documentation

To keep contributing after graduation, I documented the CNC router workflow as a fallback if new students miss training and the team’s machining knowledge is at risk. I’ve shared it with other teams, too. It covers Fusion 360 setup, workholding, CAM, and machine operation.
